# Scripture classification — working taxonomy

This is a **non-exclusive starting taxonomy**, not a complete or universally binding hierarchy.

| Candidate grouping | Examples / notes |
| --- | --- |
| Shruti / Vedic corpus | Four Vedas and associated Samhita, Brahmana, Aranyaka, Upanishad textual layers; attribution and boundaries vary |
| Smriti | Broad category used differently across traditions, including Dharmashastra and other transmitted works |
| Itihasa | Ramayana; Mahabharata (including Bhagavad Gita) |
| Puranic literature | Mahapuranas, Upapuranas, regional and other Puranic works |
| Vedanga and ancillary learning | Phonetics, ritual, grammar, etymology, metre, astronomy/astral calculation; not automatically classified as Shruti |
| Upaveda | Traditionally associated auxiliary knowledge systems; inventories and associations vary |
| Agama and Tantra | Shaiva, Vaishnava, Shakta and other lineages, with overlap and variant classifications |
| Darshana and commentaries | Sutras, Bhashyas, philosophical and exegetical works |
| Devotional literature | Stotras, hymns, saints' compositions |
| Regional texts and oral traditions | Tamil, Telugu, Kannada, Malayalam, Bengali, Marathi, Hindi and other languages; must record evidence and context |

## Taxonomy design rules

- **No forced single parent:** a work may have several legitimate categories.
- Distinguish a *category of works* from an *individual work*, *textual unit*, *recension*, *edition*, *translation*, and *commentary*.
- Record why a work is classified a certain way and whose classification is followed.
- Do not presume a fixed number of principal Upanishads or a universally agreed list of 18 Mahapuranas without labeling the tradition or source.
- Avoid confusing modern scholarly genre labels with self-descriptions of traditional texts.

## First task

Prepare a source-cited category inventory. Mark each candidate grouping as proposed until its scope and definition are reviewed.
