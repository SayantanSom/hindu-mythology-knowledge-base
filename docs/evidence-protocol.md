# Evidence and editorial protocol — draft v0.1

## Evidence chain

Work → recension/version → edition/witness → passage locator → original text (where lawful/available) → translation and attribution → explicit claim → interpretation and tradition.

## Required for a published claim

1. Stable ID and concise claim wording.
2. Source work and exact passage locator (book/chapter/verse/hymn/etc.).
3. Recension and edition identity where they affect wording or numbering.
4. Evidence excerpt or edition reference, checked by an editor.
5. Whether the claim is a direct assertion, translation, commentary, tradition-specific interpretation, or editorial comparison.
6. Reviewer, review date, and revision history.

## Differences and uncertainty

- Store incompatible genealogies or deity identities as separate source-bound claims.
- Record variant numbering and manuscript readings.
- Separate traditional attribution from modern historical scholarship.
- Never infer a unique deity identity from a shared epithet without evidence.
- Explicitly label incomplete, disputed, or unsourced conclusions.

## Language handling

Use Unicode for source script, standard transliteration (IAST where appropriate), language identifier, script identifier, and translator credits. Preserve Vedic accents where available.

## Rights

Check the rights of every digital edition, translation, manuscript scan, and image before reproducing. A public-domain ancient work does not imply a modern edition or translation is public domain.
